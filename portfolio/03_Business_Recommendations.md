# Business Recommendations

## E-Commerce Sales & Customer Analytics

This section translates the analytical findings from the project into practical business recommendations.

The recommendations are based on the completed Python analysis, SQL business analysis, RFM customer segmentation, and Power BI dashboard.

---

# 1. Protect High-Value Customers

## Business Finding

The customer analysis identified significant revenue contribution from high-value customers and RFM segments.

Key findings:

- 5,851 identified customers were included in customer-level analysis.
- 1,329 customers were classified as Champions.
- Champions contributed approximately 68.69% of customer-attributed revenue.
- 288 customers were classified as At Risk.
- Approximately £1.02M in revenue was associated with At-Risk customers.
- 13 critical/high at-risk customers were associated with approximately £398K of at-risk revenue.

## Recommendation

Use RFM-based customer monitoring to identify high-value customers whose purchasing behavior indicates increasing risk.

Customers with high historical value but weaker recent purchasing activity can be reviewed for targeted retention and engagement.

## Suggested Business Actions

- Monitor high-value customers regularly.
- Identify customers moving toward the At Risk segment.
- Create targeted retention campaigns.
- Review recent purchasing activity before customer value declines further.
- Focus customer engagement efforts on customers with meaningful revenue contribution.
- Track changes in RFM scores over time.

## Expected Business Benefit

A structured retention approach can help the business identify potentially valuable customers requiring attention before their contribution decreases.

# 2. Use Customer Segmentation for Targeted Marketing

## Business Finding

The RFM analysis divided identified customers into business-oriented segments.

Major segments include:

- Champions
- Loyal Customers
- At Risk
- Needs Attention
- Hibernating
- Potential Loyalists
- Recent Customers
- Big Spenders

The analysis shows that customer behavior and value are not evenly distributed across the customer base.

## Recommendation

Use customer segments to support differentiated customer journeys instead of treating all customers in the same way.

## Suggested Business Actions

### Champions

Maintain engagement with customers who demonstrate strong recency, frequency, and monetary characteristics.

Possible actions:

- Loyalty engagement
- Personalized offers
- Early access to selected products
- Recognition-based customer programs

### Loyal Customers

Encourage continued purchasing and strengthen long-term customer relationships.

Possible actions:

- Cross-selling
- Product recommendations
- Loyalty incentives
- Personalized communication

### Potential Loyalists

Encourage customers with promising purchasing behavior to become more frequent customers.

Possible actions:

- Follow-up promotions
- Product recommendations
- Repeat-purchase incentives

### Needs Attention

Review customers whose RFM characteristics indicate that additional engagement may be useful.

Possible actions:

- Targeted communication
- Product recommendations
- Limited promotional incentives

### At Risk

Identify customers with weaker recent engagement and meaningful historical value.

Possible actions:

- Retention campaigns
- Personalized offers
- Re-engagement communication
- Monitoring of future purchase activity

### Hibernating

Use selective re-engagement strategies while monitoring whether the segment responds to campaigns.

## Expected Business Benefit

Customer segmentation allows marketing and customer engagement activities to be aligned with observed customer behavior.

# 3. Monitor Revenue Concentration

## Business Finding

Customer revenue is concentrated among a relatively small portion of identified customers.

The analysis found:

- The top 10 customers account for approximately 16.10% of customer-attributed revenue.
- Champions contribute approximately 68.69% of customer-attributed revenue.

This indicates that a substantial portion of customer-attributed revenue is generated by high-value customer groups.

## Recommendation

Monitor customer revenue concentration as part of regular business performance reporting.

## Suggested Business Actions

- Track revenue contribution from top customers.
- Monitor changes in customer concentration over time.
- Identify dependency on a small number of high-value customers.
- Compare revenue contribution across RFM segments.
- Monitor whether revenue concentration is increasing or decreasing.
- Develop customer acquisition and retention strategies using segment-level information.

## Expected Business Benefit

Regular monitoring can help management understand how dependent overall customer revenue is on high-value customers and customer segments.

# 9. Maintain KPI Governance

## Business Finding

The project used multiple analytical layers:

**Python → SQL → Power BI**

The final transaction layer contains:

- 993,401 transaction rows
- £19.43M total revenue
- 39,492 orders
- 11,097,626 units
- 5,851 identified customers
- £492.09 average order value

The project also included cross-layer validation between the analytical outputs.

## Recommendation

Maintain consistent definitions and reconciliation checks whenever the dataset, business logic, or dashboard is updated.

## Suggested Business Actions

- Maintain a single authoritative transaction dataset.
- Document KPI definitions.
- Reconcile major KPIs after data updates.
- Validate Python outputs against SQL results.
- Validate Power BI KPIs against the approved analytical dataset.
- Document changes to business rules.
- Recheck RFM calculations when customer data or scoring logic changes.

## Expected Business Benefit

KPI governance helps maintain consistency between analytical tools and reduces the risk of reporting conflicting business metrics.
